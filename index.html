Featured Book · Novel

Loving a Broken Country

Karim's Lebanon from Civil War to Hope

Karim is six years old when the war begins. He doesn't understand the politics, the militias, or the shifting lines of a fractured country. He only knows that the radio changes, the sirens sound different, and his parents' faces carry a weight they never had before.

From a nine-story building in Beirut, Karim experiences the Lebanese Civil War through the things a child can touch and remember. But this is not only a war story: as Karim grows, the novel follows Lebanon itself through decades of fragile peace, political deals, foreign occupation, economic collapse, and the search for a country that works.

Through radios and pagers, churches and protests, family meals and funerals, it shows how ordinary people carry history in their bodies and their habits — cooking, praying, arguing, loving, and refusing to leave.

About the Author

Nicolas Hanhan is a writer and educator dedicated to exploring the intersections of culture, language, and resilience. Fluent in both English and Lebanese Arabic, his storytelling is deeply informed by the rich heritage and complex history of Lebanon. His debut novel, Loving a Broken Country, captures the enduring spirit of ordinary people navigating decades of conflict, survival, and transformation.

Beyond fiction, Nicolas is also the author of the educational guide Arabian Nights: A Conversational Guide to the Arabic Language. He holds advanced degrees in business and science, bringing a structured, analytical lens to his creative and professional pursuits. He currently resides in Arizona, where he works in higher education technology, specializing in instructional design, accessibility, and artificial intelligence integration. When he isn't writing or developing faculty training programs, he can often be found perfecting traditional Middle Eastern recipes like toum and za'atar manakish.
